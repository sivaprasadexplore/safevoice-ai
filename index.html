Your Rights Under the POSH Act 2013

▼

The Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH) Act 2013 is Indian law that protects every employee — permanent, contractual, temporary, or intern — in any workplace with 10 or more employees.

1

Right to file a complaint — You can file a complaint within 3 months of the incident. This deadline can be extended by the ICC if there is sufficient cause.

2

Right to confidentiality — Your identity, complaint, and evidence cannot be shared with anyone by the ICC. This is a legal obligation, not a courtesy.

3

Right to a fair investigation — The ICC must complete the inquiry within 90 days. You have the right to present your case and cross-examine witnesses.

4

Right to interim relief — You can request to be transferred, work from home, or have the accused transferred during the investigation — before a verdict.

5

Right against retaliation — Any retaliation for filing a complaint is itself a punishable offence under the Act. Document everything.

6

Right to appeal — If you are unhappy with the ICC's decision, you can appeal to the employer, a court, or an appellate authority within 90 days.

What counts as sexual harassment under the Act?
Unwelcome physical contact, demands for sexual favours, sexually coloured remarks, showing pornography, or any other unwelcome verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature. It also includes hostile work environment created by such behaviour.
What if my company has no ICC?
If your company has fewer than 10 employees, or if the ICC is non-functional, you can file your complaint with the Local Complaints Committee (LCC) set up by the District Officer in your area. This is mandatory under the Act.
Can I withdraw my complaint?
Yes, but only in writing. If the withdrawal is due to pressure, coercion, or threats — that coercion itself is a separate offence. SafeVoice AI monitors for exactly this pattern.

Mental Health & Emotional Support

▼

What you are going through is real, and what you are feeling is valid. Many survivors experience a range of emotions — and all of them are normal.

✓

It is not your fault. Harassment is always the responsibility of the person who commits it — never the person who receives it.

✓

You do not have to prove it alone. SafeVoice AI's evidence intelligence is designed to do the heavy lifting — you just need to share what you have.

✓

Taking your time is okay. There is no rush to decide what to do. Use this portal to document at your own pace.

Common feelings after a workplace harassment incident
Shock, self-doubt, anxiety, fear of not being believed, anger, shame, and numbness are all normal responses. These feelings do not mean you are weak — they mean you are human.
How to take care of yourself right now
Talk to someone you trust — a friend, family member, or counsellor. Write down what happened while it is fresh. Avoid being alone with the person who harassed you. Know that you have options and support.
When should I seek professional help?
If you are experiencing trouble sleeping, panic attacks, inability to focus at work, or thoughts of self-harm — please reach out to a mental health professional immediately. Free counselling is available through the helplines in this portal.
What if I'm not sure what happened to me counts as harassment?
If it made you feel uncomfortable, unsafe, or threatened — it matters. You do not need to be certain about the legal definition to reach out. Counsellors and legal advisors on the helpline can help you understand your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

▼
Will anyone find out that I reported?
Not through SafeVoice AI. Your identity is never stored — only a case token. Even system administrators cannot link your token to your name. The ICC is legally required to maintain confidentiality.
What if the accused is more senior than me?
The POSH Act protects you regardless of the accused person's seniority, influence, or position. The ICC is legally required to be independent of the management hierarchy.
What if I am a contractual employee or intern?
You are fully protected under the POSH Act. Contractual employees, interns, consultants, and temporary staff all have the same rights as permanent employees.
What if I have no evidence?
Your testimony is evidence. You do not need audio recordings or messages to file a complaint. Witness statements, your own account, and patterns of behaviour all count. ICC is obligated to investigate based on your complaint alone.
What happens to the accused during the investigation?
The accused continues working unless you request interim relief. You can request a transfer, work-from-home arrangement, or ask that the accused be kept away from your workspace during the 90-day inquiry.
Can a man file a POSH complaint?
Under the current POSH Act 2013, the law specifically protects women employees. However, many companies have internal policies that extend protection to all genders. Check your company's HR policy or consult a legal helpline.
What if my ICC is not following the process?
You can escalate to the employer, file a complaint with the District Officer, or approach a court. SafeVoice AI's process monitoring detects ICC violations in real time — your case history is timestamped evidence of any process failure.
How long does the process take?
The ICC must complete its inquiry within 90 days of receiving your complaint. It must then submit a report to the employer within 10 days of completion. The employer must act within 60 days of receiving the report.
